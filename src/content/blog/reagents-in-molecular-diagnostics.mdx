# Biochemistry Reagents in Molecular Diagnostics

Reagents in molecular diagnostics play a crucial role in various laboratory experiments and analysis. They are substances or compounds used to detect, measure, or produce other substances in biological reactions.

## Enzymes

**Purpose:** Enzymes are biological catalysts that accelerate chemical reactions in living organisms.

### DNA Polymerase
Used in PCR (Polymerase Chain Reaction) for DNA amplification.

- **Enzymatic Catalyst:** DNA Polymerase is crucial in DNA replication, synthesizing new DNA strands by adding nucleotides complementary to a template strand.
- **PCR Amplification:** In PCR, DNA Polymerase catalyzes the synthesis of complementary DNA strands, enabling exponential amplification of specific DNA regions.
- **Heat Stability:** DNA Polymerases used in PCR are selected for heat stability — withstanding the high temperatures (denaturation step) where DNA strands separate.
- **Types:** Taq Polymerase and high-fidelity Polymerases are the primary types, chosen based on accuracy requirements.

### RNAase
Used to degrade RNA in molecular biology experiments.

- **RNA Degradation:** Prevents interference with processes focused on DNA or proteins.
- **Contamination Prevention:** Prevents degradation of DNA samples by removing trace RNA.
- **Applications:** Used in nucleic acid purification protocols and laboratory sterilization.

## Buffers

**Purpose:** Buffers maintain a stable pH in a solution, preventing drastic changes in acidity or alkalinity.

### Phosphate Buffer
Commonly used in molecular biology experiments. Phosphate buffers are physiologically relevant, mimicking the pH conditions within living organisms, and are frequently employed in DNA and protein studies.

### Tris-HCl Buffer
Used in protein biochemistry. Known for effective buffering capacity in the physiological pH range, making it suitable for maintaining a constant environment for proteins during experiments.

## Substrates

### Chromogenic Substrates
Used in enzyme assays to produce a colored product. The intensity of color produced is directly proportional to enzyme activity, allowing quantitative analysis.

### Fluorogenic Substrates
Used for fluorescence-based assays. They offer high sensitivity with real-time monitoring capability and support multiplexing — simultaneous monitoring of multiple enzymatic activities.

## Indicators

### pH Indicators
Like phenolphthalein or bromothymol blue — undergo a visible color change in response to pH variations. Used in titration endpoint detection and routine pH monitoring.

### Redox Indicators
Used to monitor redox reactions. Undergo a color change in response to oxidation state changes and serve as endpoint detectors in redox titrations.

## Proteins and Antibodies

### Horseradish Peroxidase (HRP)
Used in Western blotting and ELISA. HRP catalyzes the oxidation of substrates by hydrogen peroxide, producing a detectable signal — commonly a chromogenic or fluorogenic color change.

### Green Fluorescent Protein (GFP)
Used as a fluorescent tag in cell biology. GFP is genetically fused to a target protein; when expressed in cells, the fusion protein emits green fluorescence, enabling visualization of localization and movement.

## Nucleic Acid Stains

### Ethidium Bromide
Used in gel electrophoresis to stain DNA. Intercalates between DNA base pairs and exhibits fluorescence under UV light, enabling visualization and quantification of DNA bands.

### SYBR Green
Fluorescent dye for DNA detection in real-time PCR. Selectively binds to double-stranded DNA, resulting in enhanced fluorescence proportional to the amount of DNA synthesized.

## Coenzymes and Cofactors

### NAD+ and NADH
Involved in redox reactions. NAD+ accepts electrons during metabolic processes; NADH donates electrons. They play a crucial role in energy transfer within cells during glycolysis and the citric acid cycle.

### ATP
The primary energy currency of the cell. ATP participates in muscle contraction, active transport, and synthesis of macromolecules, and has a regulatory role in energy balance.

## Inhibitors

### Protease Inhibitors
Used in protein purification. Block protease enzyme activity, preventing degradation of target proteins during purification. Common examples: PMSF, leupeptin, aprotinin, EDTA.

### ATPase Inhibitors
Studied for their role in cellular processes. ATPase inhibitors like ouabain and bafilomycin are research tools for investigating ion transport, muscle function, and cellular physiology.

## Chelating Agents

### EDTA (Ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid)
Used to chelate metal ions in molecular biology experiments. EDTA chelates divalent cations (like magnesium ions) to prevent nuclease activity and protect nucleic acids.

### Phenol-Chloroform-Isoamyl Alcohol (PCI)
Used for nucleic acid extraction. An organic reagent that separates nucleic acids from other cellular components by denaturing proteins and disrupting cellular membranes.

### Ethanol and Isopropanol
Used for DNA precipitation. When added to DNA solutions with salt, they disrupt the balance of solubility, causing DNA to precipitate for collection and purification.
